My struggle with bad customer service started well before the current AI hype.
In the best cases, it is just negligence.
In the worst ones, it is malice meant to discourage any request.
Here is what I have encountered far too many times.

- The customer service page is hidden behind a dark pattern on a page unrelated to my problem.
- I must re-enter all my information, despite the business already having it.
- The robots reroute me to the pages I have already visited, which do not address my issue.
- I have to fight a chatbot to contact a human.
- I have to fight the human to acknowledge that my issue is real.
- The human cannot fix the issue because they cannot access the right ERP system.

Here is how it is supposed to work instead.

1. I encounter a problem with the product that I cannot fix myself.
2. I find the customer service page easily.
3. If I have a frequent problem, an automated system helps me fix the issue.
4. If I have an unusual problem, I can contact a human who fixes it.

Notice how this flow does not require technologically advanced solutions.
It is mostly a managerial problem.

- Design good processes that prevent problems.
- Design a good user interface to find the customer service page and navigate possible solutions.
- Design good automations connected to all relevant ERP systems to fix the standard issues.
- Onboard humans who can easily be contacted and who are provided with the tools to fix unusual problems.

This is why I am sceptical of any integration of AI in customer service.
It can only work if the basics are covered.
For example:
- You can add retrieval-augmented generation to help find the solution to standard problems, if good documentation exists.
- You can let an agent fix an issue, if standard solutions have actually been defined.
- You can use LLMs to triage unusual problems to the right human, if human support is already in place.

If a business is integrating AI into its customer service without care, it is just wasting my time and does not deserve my money.
