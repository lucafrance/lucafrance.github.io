Mea culpa: this website [used to have]({% post_url 2022/2022-03-26-cookies-jekyll %}) a [dickover](https://daringfireball.net/2026/05/what_is_a_dickover) for one Google Analytics cookie.
It might have been benigne in the grand scheme of things, but it always bothered me.
I choose to pay for [Plausible](https://plausible.io/) for this website's analytics and happily [yanked the cookie banner away](https://github.com/lucafrance/lucafrance.github.io/commit/75631ed92cb9c87370f4a6634ad5e0938a41fef2).

This website is my digital place to share my thoughts about stuff I care about.
I choose to care about the privacy and attention of the visitors.
I choose not to throw a dickover in front of me when introducing myself digitally.

Many people share similar decisions, too many choose otherwise.

Every dickover is a choice.
Every time I see a dickover, I am reminded of someone who had a choice to be a dickhead and took it.
- A choice to get your uninformed "consent" to share your data with [hundreds of third parties](https://proton.me/blog/outlook-is-microsofts-new-data-collection-service).
- A choice to drive "engagement" with the [Ludovico technique](https://tvtropes.org/pmwiki/pmwiki.php/Main/TheLudovicoTechnique) of autoplaying videos.
- A choice to pretend to care about your "feedback" before you even had a chance to try the service.
- A choice to [spam](/assets/2026/dickover-choice/dickover-newsletter-eufy.png) a [newsletter](/assets/2026/dickover-choice/dickover-newsletter-techradar.png) to increase a metric you don't care about.

The ubiquitousness of dickovers is no excuse for them.
If you are in the position to choose, choose wisely.
